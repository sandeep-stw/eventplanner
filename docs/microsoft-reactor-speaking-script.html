How to use this script

Read the narration in your own voice. Blue boxes are actions for you, not words to read aloud. Timing includes screen navigation and the live conversation; the text alone is not a 35-minute speech.

Before rehearsal: verify the deployed agent, connected actions, live prices, stock, and save behavior. Repository topic YAML contains placeholder flow IDs. Do not overwrite working topics or promise capabilities you have not tested.

Ready tabs: existing EventPlanner solution, Copilot Studio test pane, and Dataverse Event Plans. Keep this script on a second screen if possible.

0–3 minutes · Welcome and scenario

1. Open with the business problem

DO: Show the agent's starting screen. Replace the name placeholder below before presenting.

Hello everyone, welcome to Microsoft Reactor. I'm [your name]. Today I'll show you Theme Studio, an event-planning agent built with Copilot Studio and connected to business data.

Imagine you're planning a birthday for 40 people. You know your budget, but you still need to choose a look, work out what to buy, and understand the total cost. A useful agent should help with those decisions—not just produce a nice paragraph.

We'll follow that shopper's journey: describe the event, choose a theme, review a priced shopping plan, change the budget, and approve saving the result.

This is an existing solution in my environment. I'm not building everything from scratch today. I'll show how the pieces work together and where the business data comes from.

One important boundary: when you hear “order” in this demo, it means a saved Event Plan record. We are not taking payment, reserving inventory, or submitting an order for fulfilment.

Let's first look at the three parts behind that experience.

3–6 minutes · Architecture

2. Explain the three responsibilities

DO: Open the existing EventPlanner solution. Point to its relevant components without changing them.

There are three responsibilities here. Copilot Studio manages the conversation: it asks questions, carries the shopper's choices forward, and presents the result.

The connected actions and flows handle business operations. In the flow-based design we're discussing, that includes retrieving data, calculating quantities and totals, and saving the approved plan.

Dataverse stores the structured information: the themes, the products that belong to each theme, and the saved plan and its line items.

The important design idea is separation of responsibility. A conversational model can help explain a result, but business prices and quantities should come from a controlled source and repeatable logic.

Use the actual deployed action names and implementation when showing your environment. Do not imply the repository proves the live tenant configuration.

Before we start the chat, let me show why the catalog matters.

6–9 minutes · Catalog and reuse

3. Show existing tables and the mapping

DO: Show Themes, Products, and Theme Products. Use one Tropical Luau mapping row as your example.

A Theme describes a curated look. A Product holds details such as its name, price, pack size, and stock. The Theme Product table connects the two.

That relationship is important. A product being in the catalog does not automatically mean it belongs in every theme. The mapping tells us which products are eligible for this particular look.

The mapping also provides business rules such as the item's role and quantity basis. Core items are essentials; Accent and Upgrade items are optional candidates for budget trimming.

These tables already exist in my environment. If I assemble another solution in the same environment, I can use Add existing, then Table, to include them. I do not need to create duplicate tables or import the same data again.

Adding an existing table to another solution is not making an independent copy of its records. It is still the same table, so I would avoid changing shared business data casually during a demo.

OPTIONAL: Point to Add existing → Table without completing a new solution setup live. Adding tables alone does not create or wire an agent and its actions.

Now let's see how the shopper uses this data without needing to understand the tables.

9–15 minutes · Start the conversation

4. Discover the event and choose a theme

DO: Start a fresh test conversation. Type Plan a birthday for 40 people. Answer the actual prompts with Birthday, 40 guests, Bright & Tropical, and budget 400. Pause for responses.

I'll use a simple scenario: a birthday for 40 guests, a bright tropical look, and a budget of 400 pounds.

Notice how the conversation gathers structured requirements. Occasion, guest count, vibe, and budget are not just descriptive words; they become inputs for the next operations.

I'll answer one question at a time. That makes it easier to see what the agent has collected and keeps this demonstration predictable.

WAIT: Let recommendations appear. Point to an actual returned theme. Select Tropical Luau if it is returned, as confirmed during rehearsal.

Here are the themes the agent returned for our requirements. At this stage, a theme estimate is guidance; the detailed shopping plan gives us the itemised amount.

I'll choose Tropical Luau. That selection gives the next operation a specific theme to work with, rather than asking it to combine unrelated products from across the catalog.

Now let's look at the difference between recommending a theme and pricing an actual plan.

15–21 minutes · Shopping list and budget rules

5. Explain the returned plan

DO: Wait for the shopping list. Point to one product's quantity, unit price, and line total, then the total and any exclusions. Use the numbers actually displayed.

We now have an itemised plan. Instead of simply saying “buy some plates and decorations,” the result tells us the products, quantities, unit prices, and line totals.

For packaged products, the required quantity needs to respect pack size and rounding. For other products, the quantity can depend on the event or number of tables. These are business rules, not creative guesses.

Next, look at the budget. The intended rule is to remove optional items before essential ones and explain what was left out. If the essentials themselves exceed the budget, the agent should say so rather than claim the plan fits.

Also notice the setup estimate. Assembly time is not the duration of the party; those are different things.

Only if verified: for the sample £400 Tropical Luau case, reference figures are £379.82, 3.5 setup hours, and 11 included items, with the Neon LED Sign excluded. If your live result differs, do not read these figures as facts.

At this point the shopper has enough information to decide. We haven't charged anything, and we haven't saved an approved order yet.

DO: Choose Not yet at the approval prompt before moving to the adjustment, unless your rehearsed live interaction handles the change directly.

Requirements often change. Let's see what happens when the shopper changes the budget.

21–25 minutes · Change a requirement

6. Reprice the selected theme

DO: Say Change my budget, choose My budget if prompted, and enter 600. Use the precise interaction rehearsed with your deployed agent.

I'll increase the budget to 600 pounds. We are still planning the same event and working with the same selected theme.

The point is not to ask the model to invent a higher price. We rerun the planning logic with a changed input and show the new result.

WAIT: Point out changes actually shown. Then defer saving, change the budget back to 400, and review the newly displayed total.

You can compare the new result with the previous plan. If optional products are now included, we can see their effect on the total.

I'll return to the original 400-pound budget for our final approved plan. We will approve the current result, not an earlier amount from the conversation.

Now we can move from discussing the plan to saving the approved result.

25–29 minutes · Save and verify

7. Approve the amount and inspect the records

DO: Check the displayed amount. Choose Yes, create my order in the demo environment. Wait for the result; note the returned plan number.

The agent is asking me to approve the displayed total. This is the intended confirmation point before creating the saved plan.

I am approving a demo Event Plan, not a payment or fulfilment order. Let's wait for the result and then verify it in Dataverse.

DO: Open Event Plans. Find the exact returned plan number and show its related lines. Verify the saved total, status, included products, and line count. If excluded lines are stored too, distinguish total saved lines from included lines.

Here is the saved record identified by the plan number. We can inspect the header and the line items instead of relying only on the chat message.

The verification matters: the approved products and amount should match the saved result. If this environment stores excluded items as well, those must remain clearly marked and must not be confused with included items.

This is what turns the conversation into a business workflow: a useful result that can be inspected and followed up.

Do not claim the saved count or amount matches until you check it. Do not retry a save blindly if you cannot find the record.

29–30 minutes · Close the demonstration

8. Deliver three takeaways

Let me leave you with three takeaways.

First, ground the experience in trusted business data. Here, the theme-product mapping defines the curated assortment.

Second, use repeatable logic for business calculations. Prices and quantities should not depend on a model improvising an answer.

Third, make the action boundary visible. The shopper reviews the amount, approves the plan, and we verify the saved records.

Today we used the existing EventPlanner solution rather than rebuilding it. We stopped at the saved plan; payment, stock reservation, and fulfilment are separate integrations.

Thank you. I'd love to take your questions.

30–35 minutes · Audience Q&A

Ready-to-use answers

Can this connect to Business Central, Finance & Operations, or CRM?

Yes, that is a possible extension, but those integrations are not demonstrated here. Dynamics 365 Sales uses Dataverse, while Business Central and Finance & Operations integrations can use suitable connectors or APIs. We would first decide which system owns products, prices, inventory, customers, and orders, then configure the actions, permissions, and error handling. A saved Event Plan is not automatically a posted ERP transaction.

Can I reuse my existing tables?

Yes. In the same environment, use Add existing, then Table, when assembling another solution. That reuses the existing table rather than cloning it. You still need to connect the agent's actions correctly; adding tables alone does not do that.

Does this agent take payment or reserve stock?

No, not in this demo. Those are separate business operations that need their own integrations, validation, and confirmation steps.

What would you add before production?

I would review authentication, permissions, data privacy, connection ownership, monitoring, and failure recovery. I would also prevent duplicate saves on retries and verify that the complete set of approved line items is saved before reporting success.

If nobody asks a question

One question worth considering is: where should the agent stop, and where should your system of record take over? For this demo, the stopping point is a saved Event Plan. Your business might choose a quote or another approval process as the next step.

Backup words if the live demo fails

An action fails or times out

The live integration hasn't completed successfully, so I won't claim that the operation succeeded. I'll switch to a recording from rehearsal to show the intended journey, and we can still inspect the architecture. The live failure needs investigation after the session.

The conversation goes off-script

Let me reset to our prepared scenario so we can clearly follow the inputs and outputs.

The price differs from rehearsal

This amount differs from the rehearsed result. I won't approve saving until I've checked the inputs and current catalog data. For now, I'll explain the expected workflow using the prepared example.

A saved record is not visible

I'll check using the returned plan number before trying again. Repeating a save without checking could create a duplicate record.

Time running short? Skip optional theme details, stock changes, and deep flow-designer navigation. Prioritize the priced result, explicit approval, record verification, and closing takeaways.
